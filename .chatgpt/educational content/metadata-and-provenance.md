# Educational metadata and provenance

Metadata makes educational content searchable, auditable, reusable and maintainable. Metadata must describe the content accurately; it must not be used to disguise missing educational review.

## Required metadata

Every content record must include, as applicable:

- Stable identifier and version.
- Content type and supported interaction/task type.
- Title/internal label and publication status.
- Primary skill and specific topic/subskill tags.
- Intended CEFR level or curriculum stage.
- CEFR/curriculum rationale reference.
- Working difficulty label and an explicit indication of whether it is editorial or empirically calibrated.
- Learning objective.
- Author/editor and creation/update dates.
- Reviewer identity, qualification/role, review date and decision.
- Source/provenance and rights status.
- Related content identifiers (passage, question, answer key, explanation, form).
- Locale/language of instructions and target-language content.
- Accessibility notes or known limitations.
- Version history and retirement/replacement relationship.

Only store fields relevant to the content type, but do not omit required educational information.

## Metadata quality rules

- Use controlled vocabularies for skills, topics, levels, difficulty and status.
- Use specific topic tags. Generic labels such as “Grammar” or “Reading” are not sufficient by themselves.
- Keep one primary skill per item; use secondary tags only when they represent genuine additional coverage.
- Ensure the CEFR level is consistent across linked content, or document why linked material differs.
- Distinguish editorial difficulty from pilot-derived item statistics.
- Never mark a draft as approved because it has an author, timestamp or generated content.
- Keep provenance for copied, adapted, translated or AI-assisted content.
- Preserve the exact reviewed version so approval can be traced to the content learners saw.

## Provenance for AI-assisted content

Where AI tools contributed materially, retain the tool/workflow and the human editor responsible for the final version according to the project's internal recordkeeping policy. AI generation is not a reviewer qualification and is not evidence of correctness.

## Automated validation

Automated checks should verify required fields, controlled values, valid relationships, version consistency and likely duplicate content. These checks establish record integrity only.

## Release blockers

Missing identifiers/versions, invalid controlled values, orphaned relationships, generic topic tagging, unknown rights status, untraceable review decisions or inaccurate metadata block publication.
