# Answer keys and distractors

Answer keys determine what the system treats as correct. Distractors are incorrect response options used to make a task diagnostically useful. Errors here directly teach the wrong thing or unfairly penalize learners.

## Answer-key requirements

For every scored item, record:

- The correct option identifier or canonical expected answer.
- The complete accepted-answer set for text-entry items, including legitimate spelling, punctuation, contraction or word-order variants where applicable.
- The scoring rule, including partial-credit behavior if supported.
- A concise justification showing why the key is correct in the context of the prompt and any passage.
- Any normalization applied before comparison.
- The reviewer and review date for the key.
- A version relationship to the item it belongs to.

The key must be independently checked against the full prompt and source material, not just copied from the author's intended answer.

## Distractor requirements

For selected-response items, each distractor must:

1. Be clearly incorrect in the given context.
2. Be plausible to a learner at the target level.
3. Reflect a realistic misconception, transfer error or incomplete understanding where possible.
4. Be grammatically and semantically parallel to the correct option.
5. Avoid overlap with another option or a second defensible interpretation.
6. Avoid absurd, humorous or obviously unrelated alternatives unless the task explicitly teaches recognition of that contrast.
7. Avoid clues based on option length, grammar, specificity, capitalization or formatting.
8. Avoid “all/none of the above” unless there is a documented pedagogical reason and the implementation supports it reliably.

For each distractor, retain an internal rationale describing the misconception or error it is intended to reveal. Do not expose that rationale before the learner submits an answer if it would reveal the key.

## Review checklist

A qualified teacher must verify:

- The key is correct and uniquely defensible.
- Every accepted variant is genuinely acceptable in context.
- Every distractor is wrong in context, but plausible for the intended learner.
- No distractor becomes correct under a reasonable interpretation.
- The key does not depend on an unstated convention.
- The scoring rule matches the task instructions.
- Option positions do not create a predictable answer pattern across a form.

Core grammar and high-impact placement keys require a second independent teacher review.

## Empirical checks

After an appropriately designed pilot, examine:

- The proportion of learners selecting each option.
- Whether distractors attract the intended lower-proficiency responses.
- Whether a distractor is never selected or is selected by stronger learners disproportionately.
- Whether the item discriminates meaningfully between learners with different levels of the target ability.

These statistics inform review; they do not automatically prove that a key or distractor is educationally sound. Small or unrepresentative samples must be labelled inconclusive.

## Release blockers

Missing keys, unverified accepted variants, multiple defensible answers, unsupported scoring behavior or unresolved key-review disagreement block publication of a scored item.
