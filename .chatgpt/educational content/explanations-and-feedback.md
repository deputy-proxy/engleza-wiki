# Explanations, hints and learner feedback

Explanations should help learners understand the language point and improve future performance. Merely revealing the correct option is not an adequate explanation.

## Required explanation structure

For each scored item, provide:

- **Direct answer:** identify the correct answer after submission.
- **Reason:** explain the relevant grammar, vocabulary meaning, reading evidence or language convention.
- **Contextual application:** connect the rule to the exact sentence, passage or task.
- **Contrast:** where useful, explain why the most tempting distractor is not correct.
- **Example:** include a short additional example when it improves understanding.
- **Level-appropriate language:** explain concepts in language the intended learner can understand.

Not every explanation needs every element, but the reason and its connection to the item are mandatory.

## Quality requirements

An explanation must be:

1. Linguistically accurate and consistent with the approved answer key.
2. Specific to the item, not a generic definition pasted across unrelated questions.
3. Clear, concise and instructionally useful.
4. Free from circular reasoning (for example, “this is correct because it is the correct tense”).
5. Honest about legitimate variation and exceptions.
6. Consistent with the intended CEFR level and the course's terminology.
7. Written in natural English for target-language examples.
8. Free of unsupported rules such as “always” or “never” when real usage has exceptions.
9. Accessible and readable on mobile.
10. Reviewed whenever the prompt, key or relevant source material changes.

Romanian support text may be used where the product design calls for it, but it must preserve the pedagogical meaning and must not contradict the English explanation.

## Hints and feedback

Hints should guide attention without giving away the answer. They should:

- Point to the relevant concept, clue or part of the passage.
- Avoid exposing the correct option or a near-identical answer.
- Be optional where the task is intended to measure unaided performance.
- Be clearly separated from post-answer explanations.

Correct/incorrect feedback should be respectful and informative. Avoid shaming language, exaggerated praise, or claims about a learner's overall ability based on one response.

## Review and evidence

A qualified English-language teacher must review explanations for correctness, clarity, level appropriateness and consistency with the key. Record reviewer, role/qualification, date, decision and any required changes.

AI-generated or automatically generated explanations remain drafts until reviewed. A successful content-generation process or grammar checker is not an educational approval.

## Release blockers

An explanation blocks publication when it is missing where required, contradicts the key, teaches an incorrect rule, fails to explain the answer, or uses language substantially above the intended learner level without support.
