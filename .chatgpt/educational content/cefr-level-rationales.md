# CEFR level assignment and rationales

A CEFR label is a claim about the language ability or demand represented by content. It must be justified, not guessed from a topic name or from the author's intuition alone.

## Required level record

For each item or learning unit, record:

- Assigned CEFR level (or a clearly named non-CEFR curriculum stage).
- The specific communicative activity, skill or language feature being targeted.
- The relevant CEFR Companion Volume descriptor(s), with edition/version and a precise reference where available.
- The internal course/curriculum objective mapped to that descriptor.
- A short rationale explaining why the task demand is appropriate for the assigned level.
- Any assumptions about learner knowledge or context.
- Reviewer identity, qualification/role, date and decision.
- A note where the task samples only a narrow subskill rather than broad proficiency.

Do not fabricate descriptor quotations or references. Prefer a faithful paraphrase with a precise descriptor reference.

## How to judge level

Consider the combined demand of:

- Vocabulary range, frequency and abstraction.
- Grammar structures and complexity.
- Text length, cohesion, density and discourse structure.
- Reading/listening processing required, where applicable.
- Amount of inference, implied meaning and pragmatic interpretation.
- Task instructions and response complexity.
- Contextual familiarity and support provided to the learner.

Assign the level based on the hardest essential demand needed to complete the task successfully. A familiar topic does not automatically make advanced language easy, and a grammar label does not automatically determine a CEFR level.

## Scope and limits

A single item can provide evidence about a narrow skill or language feature. It cannot establish a learner's overall CEFR level.

A form that samples grammar, vocabulary and reading only must not claim to measure listening, speaking or writing. Focused practice exercises must not be described as comprehensive placement assessments.

Use “indicative estimate” for learner-facing placement outcomes unless a documented validation study supports a stronger claim. Do not call an internal test an official CEFR exam or certificate.

## Review requirements

A qualified English-language teacher with CEFR assessment familiarity must review every level assignment and rationale. A second independent reviewer is recommended for high-impact placement items and required by the project's review policy for core grammar/high-impact placement content.

Disagreements must be recorded and resolved with evidence. Do not average conflicting level labels mechanically.

## Release blockers

Missing or generic rationales, unsupported descriptor mappings, level labels based only on topic names, unresolved reviewer disagreement or claims broader than the measured skill block approval.
