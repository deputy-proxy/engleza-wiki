# Assessment forms and blueprints

A form is a curated set of items assembled to serve a defined instructional or assessment purpose. A blueprint describes what the form is intended to sample and how that sampling is distributed.

## Required form record

Each form must define:

- Stable form identifier, title and version.
- Form purpose: placement, diagnostic, formative practice, summative assessment or another explicit purpose.
- Intended learner population and prerequisite assumptions.
- Intended CEFR level or curriculum stage, if applicable.
- Skills, subskills and topics covered, and important exclusions.
- Item count and section composition.
- Target distribution of item types and working difficulty.
- Time estimate and administration conditions.
- Selection rules and item-pool requirements.
- Scoring model and result interpretation.
- Accessibility and accommodation considerations.
- Form author/editor, teacher reviewers, review dates and approval status.
- Version history and the exact item versions included.

## Blueprint requirements

The blueprint must make it possible to determine whether the form actually samples its stated objectives. It should specify:

1. The construct and intended use of the form.
2. Skills and subskills included and excluded.
3. Coverage targets by skill/topic.
4. Item counts and distribution.
5. Difficulty targets, clearly labelled as editorial until empirically calibrated.
6. Expected completion time and rationale.
7. Rules for avoiding excessive item reuse and cueing.
8. Scoring and feedback approach.
9. Required review and pilot evidence.
10. Limitations that must be disclosed to learners.

## Form construction

- Use a sufficiently large, level-appropriate pool; do not rely on a small set of repeated prompts across forms.
- Avoid duplicate or near-duplicate prompts within a form and unintended reuse across distinct forms.
- Ensure every section contributes to the stated purpose.
- Balance correct-option positions without distorting item quality.
- Do not combine formal assessment and adaptive practice pools in a way that exposes formal items or keys.
- Keep the form version attached to every attempt so historical results remain interpretable.
- Re-review the form whenever its composition or blueprint changes.

## Placement-specific limits

The current A1-B2 placement forms are short formative screens covering grammar, vocabulary and reading. They do not measure listening, speaking or writing and cannot alone establish a complete CEFR proficiency level.

Focused practice forms teach or practise a narrower objective and must not be represented as placement tests.

## Review and pilot gates

A qualified teacher must review the blueprint, item selection, coverage and learner instructions. Each included item must independently meet the item, key, explanation and level-rationale standards.

Before making stronger claims about placement accuracy, pilot the form with a representative sample and document the method, outcomes, limitations and comparison measure. Passing an automated blueprint audit is not validation.

## Release blockers

A form cannot be approved if it materially misses its blueprint, relies on unreviewed items, has unresolved duplicate/reuse issues, lacks a scoring definition, misrepresents its scope or makes unsupported proficiency claims.
