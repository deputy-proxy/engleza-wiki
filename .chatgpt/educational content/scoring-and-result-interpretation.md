# Scoring and result interpretation

Scoring converts learner responses into a score, feedback or placement estimate. The rules must be explicit, reproducible and aligned with what the form actually measures.

## Required scoring specification

Document:

- Which responses receive credit and why.
- Points assigned per item and any weighting.
- Partial-credit rules, if supported.
- Treatment of unanswered, invalid or technically interrupted responses.
- Accepted text-entry variants and normalization behavior.
- Score aggregation and rounding rules.
- Section-level score calculations, where shown.
- Any thresholds or bands and the evidence used to set them.
- Whether feedback is formative, diagnostic or used for placement.
- Version of the scoring algorithm and form to which it applies.

The same response set and scoring version must produce the same result.

## Educational requirements

- Scoring must reward the target ability, not unrelated interface or test-taking tricks.
- Weighting must be justified by the blueprint and intended use.
- Do not imply that a percentage score is itself a CEFR level.
- Do not present editorial difficulty labels as calibrated statistical values.
- Do not infer unmeasured skills from measured ones.
- Explain uncertainty and limitations in learner-facing language.
- Keep formal assessment results separate from practice-session progress.
- Never silently recalculate historical attempts after a scoring change.

## Result language

For the current placement screen, describe the outcome as an **indicative estimate based on grammar, vocabulary and reading questions**. Clearly state that listening, speaking and writing are not assessed.

Do not use “certified”, “official CEFR result”, “CEFR qualification” or equivalent claims unless the product has the appropriate external recognition and evidence.

A single short test cannot provide a definitive measure of a learner's full language proficiency. Recommend further teacher assessment where a high-stakes decision is involved.

## Validation evidence

Before setting score bands or making placement-accuracy claims, define the intended population, pilot protocol, sample-size rationale and analysis plan. Where data support it, examine score distributions, reliability, item performance and agreement with an independent placement measure or qualified teacher judgement.

Report sample characteristics, uncertainty and limitations. Do not claim validation from a small convenience sample or from internal consistency alone.

## Release blockers

Unspecified scoring behavior, irreproducible results, unsupported thresholds, claims beyond the measured construct or missing learner-facing limitations block release.
