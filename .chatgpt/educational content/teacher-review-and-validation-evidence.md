# Teacher review and validation evidence

This document defines the human review and empirical evidence needed before educational content can be approved. It distinguishes editorial review from learner piloting and formal validation.

## Teacher qualifications and independence

Every scored item must be reviewed by a qualified English-language teacher with relevant teaching experience and competence in the content area. Reviewers must be able to assess the target CEFR level where a CEFR claim is made.

Core grammar and high-impact placement items require a second independent teacher review. The second reviewer must make an independent judgement rather than merely confirm the first reviewer's decision.

Record the reviewer's real identity, role/qualification, date, decision and evidence. Do not fabricate identities, qualifications, signatures or approval dates.

## Required review scope

Reviewers must inspect the exact version intended for release, including:

- Learning objective and intended construct.
- Prompt, instructions and learner-facing wording.
- Linguistic correctness and naturalness.
- CEFR/curriculum level fit and rationale.
- Answer key and accepted variants.
- Distractors and their plausibility.
- Explanation, hint and feedback quality.
- Reading passage, answer evidence and source alignment.
- Topic/skill/difficulty metadata.
- Fairness, accessibility and likely unintended interpretations.

A reviewer must record one of: approved, approved with specified non-blocking edits, or rejected/changes required. Comments must explain the decision. Changes to material content require a new review of the changed version.

## Editorial and technical checks

After teacher review, an editor/publisher verifies completeness, provenance, rights, accessibility, translations, metadata and version traceability.

Automated validation should check schema integrity, required fields, duplicate prompts, item/form relationships, blueprint coverage and scoring consistency. Passing automated checks is not teacher approval.

## Learner pilot

Before a form is used to make meaningful placement decisions or stronger accuracy claims:

1. Define the target population, intended use and decisions informed by results.
2. Establish recruitment criteria, consent/privacy handling and administration conditions.
3. Set a sample-size target and analysis plan with an assessment specialist before collecting responses.
4. Include learners across the intended level and adjacent levels; document cohort characteristics and limitations.
5. Record completion, abandonment, learner questions and any administration problems.
6. Analyse item difficulty, discrimination and distractor performance where the sample supports it.
7. Examine score distributions and reliability where appropriate.
8. Compare results with an independent placement measure or qualified teacher judgement.
9. Investigate weak, ambiguous or unexpected items; revise and repeat review/piloting as needed.
10. Publish a concise report describing methods, results, uncertainty and limitations.

A small convenience sample can identify usability problems but cannot establish psychometric validity. Do not report unsupported statistics or generalize beyond the sample.

## Evidence package

For every approved release, retain:

- Exact form and item versions reviewed.
- Completed review records and reviewer qualifications/roles.
- Resolved issue log and change history.
- Automated audit output and date/version.
- Source and rights records.
- Accessibility/editorial sign-off.
- Pilot protocol and report, if a pilot is required for the intended claim.
- Final release decision and approver.

## Approval states

- **Draft:** no approval implied.
- **In review:** review is underway.
- **Teacher-reviewed:** all required teacher reviews for the exact version are recorded.
- **Pilot-tested:** pilot completed and evidence retained; findings may still require changes.
- **Educationally validated:** all applicable human review, pilot/validation, editorial and release criteria for the stated use are met.
- **Rejected / changes required:** not publishable in current form.
- **Retired:** no longer used for new learner activity.

Do not equate “teacher-reviewed” with “psychometrically validated”. Validation is always relative to a defined purpose, population and use.

## Final release gate

Content is educationally validated only when:

- All applicable content-type standards are met.
- Required teacher reviews are complete and approved.
- No blocking educational, legal, accessibility or technical issues remain.
- The form blueprint and scoring rules are approved.
- Required pilot/validation evidence exists for the claims and decisions being made.
- Learner-facing wording accurately communicates scope and limitations.
- The exact approved versions and decision are traceable.

If evidence is insufficient, retain a narrower status and make narrower claims. Do not promote content to validated to satisfy a release checklist.
