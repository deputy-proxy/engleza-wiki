# Assessment items

An assessment item is a scored or unscored question, prompt or task presented to a learner. Every item must measure a defined learning objective rather than merely look like a plausible exercise.

## Required fields

Every item must have:

- A stable unique identifier and version.
- A concise title or internal label.
- A complete learner-facing prompt, with all necessary context and instructions.
- One primary learning/assessment objective.
- One primary skill (for example grammar, vocabulary or reading) and at least one specific topic or subskill.
- An intended CEFR level or explicitly named curriculum stage.
- A written rationale for the level assignment.
- A supported item/task type and a declared response format.
- A working difficulty judgement (easy, medium or hard), clearly treated as editorial rather than statistically calibrated unless pilot evidence exists.
- The expected response format and any constraints, such as word limit or accepted input form.
- A linked answer key or scoring rubric, where the item is scored.
- A linked explanation or feedback entry, where pedagogically appropriate.
- Author, creation date, status and version history.
- Source/provenance information for any borrowed or adapted material.

## Quality requirements

A publishable item must:

1. Assess one principal objective. Avoid combining unrelated grammar, vocabulary and reading demands in a way that makes the result uninterpretable.
2. Use natural, current English and a context that does not require specialist knowledge, local trivia or culturally narrow assumptions.
3. Match the intended level in the hardest essential language and reasoning demand, not merely in the topic label.
4. Be self-contained. A learner must not need unstated assumptions to identify what is being asked.
5. Have one defensible answer or a documented scoring rubric that handles all valid responses.
6. Avoid trick wording, accidental clues, unnecessary complexity and ambiguity.
7. Avoid giving away the answer through grammar, length, formatting or option patterns.
8. Be fair and inclusive. Do not rely on stereotypes, sensitive personal disclosures or irrelevant demographic knowledge.
9. Be readable and usable on supported screen sizes and with keyboard and assistive technology.
10. Be original or appropriately licensed, with source and adaptation details retained.

## Question-type requirements

Apply the requirements relevant to the implemented type:

- **Single-choice:** one unambiguously best answer; options are parallel in grammar, length and style.
- **Multiple-select:** state clearly whether one or several answers are expected; define the scoring policy for partial selections.
- **Gap-fill / cloze:** provide enough context to constrain the expected answer; document accepted variants.
- **Text entry:** specify normalization rules for case, punctuation, contractions, spelling variants and whitespace. Do not reject a linguistically valid variant accidentally.
- **Matching / ordering:** define the complete set of elements and ensure the task has a defensible solution.
- **Reading comprehension:** the answer must be supported by the supplied passage; distinguish literal retrieval from inference and interpretation.

Do not introduce a new item type until its learner instructions, answer representation, scoring behavior, accessibility and automated validation are defined.

## Evidence required before approval

- Completed item record and metadata.
- Qualified English-language teacher review of the prompt, objective, level fit and linguistic correctness.
- Review of answer key, distractors and explanation under their respective standards.
- Second independent review for core grammar and high-impact placement items.
- Resolved editorial and accessibility issues.

## Rejection conditions

Reject or return an item for revision if it has an ambiguous objective, multiple unintended answers, unsupported level assignment, unnatural language, irrelevant cultural knowledge, missing provenance, inaccessible interaction, or an answer that cannot be justified from the prompt/material.

An automated bank audit can flag missing fields, duplicate prompts and structural defects. Passing that audit does not approve the item.
