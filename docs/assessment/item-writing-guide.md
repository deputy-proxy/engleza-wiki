# Assessment item-writing and review guide

## Required item record

Every new scored item must include:

- one clear, unambiguous prompt and a single assessable objective;
- an assigned CEFR level with a short rationale tied to the CEFR Companion Volume or the internal curriculum objective; store the rationale in `metadata.cefr_rationale`;
- one primary skill and at least one specific topic tag;
- a question type and working difficulty label;
- a verified answer key and a concise explanation that teaches the relevant point;
- plausible, grammatically parallel distractors that reflect realistic learner errors;
- source/provenance notes for any adapted passage, quotation or media;
- reviewer identity, review date, decision and evidence/rationale.

## Writing rules

1. Assess one main point at a time. Avoid testing unrelated vocabulary or cultural knowledge unless it is part of the stated objective.
2. Use natural contemporary English and contexts that do not depend on local knowledge, stereotypes or specialist background knowledge.
3. Keep the language demand within the assigned level. Judge the hardest essential language/task demand, not the topic label.
4. Make every distractor plausible but clearly incorrect in context. Avoid trick questions, overlapping options, grammatical clues and answer-length clues.
5. For reading, ensure the answer is supported by the passage. Label adapted texts and retain source/permission details.
6. For text-entry and structured items, define accepted variants and validate case, punctuation and whitespace handling deliberately.
7. Provide a meaningful explanation for the key. Do not merely repeat the correct option.
8. Review the complete learner presentation on desktop and mobile, including keyboard and screen-reader usability.
9. Keep English target-language examples in English. Romanian support text must preserve the pedagogical meaning rather than translate word-for-word.

## Review requirements

- A qualified English-language teacher must review every scored item, its answer key, explanation and CEFR fit.
- Core grammar and high-impact placement items require a second independent teacher review.
- The editor/publisher checks completeness, accessibility, source/rights evidence and metadata after teacher review.
- Any change to the prompt, options, key, explanation, level, skill or topic requires review again.
- Automated validation can find structural problems and likely duplicates. It cannot approve educational correctness or substitute for a teacher.

## Review record

The review record must identify the reviewer, qualification/role, date, decision and concise evidence. For a changed answer key or CEFR assignment, record the reason for the change. Do not invent reviewer identities or mark generated drafts as teacher-reviewed.
