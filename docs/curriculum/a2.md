# A2 learning path: Confident Everyday Communication

**CEFR target:** A2  
**Entry assumption:** The learner can manage simple routine exchanges and short familiar texts, but needs support with detail and connected language.  
**Exit outcome:** The learner can communicate in routine tasks, describe immediate needs and familiar matters, and use a series of simple sentences to describe background, surroundings and immediate concerns.  
**Path structure:** 5 units × 4 lessons; one reviewed unit checkpoint per unit.

## Unit 1 — Personal experiences and relationships

**Unit outcome:** Describe people and experiences with simple connected language and ask follow-up questions.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Personality and appearance | Describe a familiar person using several relevant details. | Adjectives, be/have, basic adjective order |
| 2 | Life events and milestones | Describe a small number of past life events. | Past simple regular/irregular forms |
| 3 | Questions about the past | Ask and answer questions about a completed event. | Past simple questions/negatives with did |
| 4 | A person who matters to me | Write and present a short connected description of a person and shared experience. | Paragraph sequence, time markers, review |

**Checkpoint:** Read/listen to a short personal account and produce a short description with understandable sequencing.

## Unit 2 — Travel, transport and directions

**Unit outcome:** Handle predictable travel situations and describe a journey.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Planning a journey | Identify key details in a simple travel timetable or booking. | Transport, dates, times, going to for plans |
| 2 | At the station and airport | Ask for and understand basic travel information. | Functional questions, platform/gate/ticket vocabulary |
| 3 | Directions and location | Give and follow directions across a familiar area. | Imperatives, prepositions of movement, landmarks |
| 4 | A trip I took | Describe a past trip with a clear beginning, middle and end. | Past simple, sequencing and travel vocabulary |

**Checkpoint:** Interpret a simple itinerary, identify relevant travel details and describe a journey.

## Unit 3 — Health, services and everyday problems

**Unit outcome:** Explain a simple problem and ask for practical help in familiar service contexts.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Body and common symptoms | Describe a common symptom and say how you feel. | Body vocabulary, have/has, feel, should |
| 2 | At the pharmacy or doctor's | Explain a basic problem and understand simple advice. | Functional questions, frequency and duration |
| 3 | Problems at home and work | Explain a familiar problem and request assistance. | There is/are, can/could, basic obligation |
| 4 | Giving simple advice | Give and respond to practical advice about a familiar issue. | should/shouldn't, have to, short explanations |

**Checkpoint:** Complete a short service dialogue and understand a simple set of instructions. Medical content must remain language practice, not clinical advice.

## Unit 4 — Work, study and communication

**Unit outcome:** Describe routine responsibilities and communicate simple information about work or study.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jobs and responsibilities | Describe a job or study routine using connected sentences. | Job/study vocabulary, present simple |
| 2 | What is happening now? | Distinguish a routine from an action happening now. | Present simple vs present continuous |
| 3 | Arrangements and plans | Describe a near-future arrangement and ask about another person's plans. | Present continuous for arrangements, going to |
| 4 | A simple work or study email | Write a short message with a clear purpose and appropriate greeting/closing. | Email conventions, requests, basic connectors |

**Checkpoint:** Understand a short workplace/study message and write an appropriate short reply.

## Unit 5 — Experiences, comparisons and independent interaction

**Unit outcome:** Compare familiar options, describe experiences and sustain a short routine conversation.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Comparing places and things | Compare two familiar options using clear simple comparisons. | Comparative/superlative forms |
| 2 | Experiences so far | Ask and answer about life experiences without requiring a specific finished time. | Present perfect for experience; ever/never |
| 3 | Making plans together | Suggest, accept and decline a simple plan and give a reason. | Let's / How about / because |
| 4 | An everyday conversation | Combine descriptions, experiences, comparisons and plans in a short exchange. | Turn-taking, clarification, recycled grammar |

**Checkpoint:** Integrated task combining a short text/audio, a comparison and a brief interaction. The assessment must distinguish present-perfect experience from past-simple finished-time narratives.

## A2 assessment blueprint

Each unit checkpoint should combine receptive comprehension with controlled and short productive tasks. The final path review should sample routine interaction, past events, plans, comparisons and practical requests. Texts may be longer and less scaffolded than A1 but should remain concrete and familiar. Ensure the assessment does not rely on one memorised grammar pattern.
