# B1 learning path: Independent Communication

**CEFR target:** B1  
**Entry assumption:** The learner can manage most familiar situations and produce simple connected text, but may struggle to sustain detail, justify opinions or handle less predictable language.  
**Exit outcome:** The learner can deal with most situations likely to arise while travelling, produce connected text on familiar topics, describe experiences and events, and briefly give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans.  
**Path structure:** 5 units × 4 lessons; one reviewed unit checkpoint per unit.

## Unit 1 — Narratives, memories and life experience

**Unit outcome:** Tell a coherent story and distinguish finished past events from experiences connected to the present.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Building a clear narrative | Organise a personal narrative with a clear sequence and relevant detail. | Past simple, sequencing, paragraph focus |
| 2 | Background and interrupted events | Describe background situations and events in progress in a past narrative. | Past continuous vs past simple |
| 3 | Experience and recent results | Discuss life experience and recent events with present relevance. | Present perfect vs past simple; already/yet/just |
| 4 | A meaningful experience | Produce a connected spoken or written narrative with an appropriate tense contrast. | Cohesion, time reference, editing |

**Checkpoint:** Interpret a narrative and produce a coherent account with accurate time reference.

## Unit 2 — Opinions, reasons and discussion

**Unit outcome:** Express an opinion, support it with reasons and respond to a different view in a familiar discussion.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stating and qualifying opinions | Express an opinion and indicate its strength or uncertainty. | Opinion phrases, stance adverbs, hedging basics |
| 2 | Giving reasons and examples | Support a claim with relevant reasons and examples. | because, since, for example, such as |
| 3 | Agreeing and disagreeing constructively | Respond to another view and signal partial agreement or disagreement. | Discourse markers, polite disagreement |
| 4 | A short argument | Write a structured paragraph presenting a position and supporting reasons. | Topic sentences, cohesion, paragraph structure |

**Checkpoint:** Read/listen to two viewpoints and produce a reasoned response. Scoring should reward relevance and coherence, not agreement with a preferred opinion.

## Unit 3 — Work, learning and problem-solving

**Unit outcome:** Explain a work/study process, describe a problem and propose a practical solution.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Responsibilities and achievements | Describe responsibilities and explain a completed achievement. | Present perfect/past simple, work/study lexis |
| 2 | Explaining processes | Describe a familiar process in a logical order. | Passive voice in common present/past forms, sequencing |
| 3 | Problems and possible solutions | Explain a problem and compare possible solutions. | First conditional, modals of advice/possibility |
| 4 | A professional message | Write a clear email describing a problem, its impact and a requested action. | Register, paragraphing, requests and follow-up |

**Checkpoint:** Interpret a workplace/study scenario and write a practical response with a justified recommendation.

## Unit 4 — Future choices and hypothetical situations

**Unit outcome:** Discuss plans, predictions and realistic consequences, and describe imagined or unlikely situations.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Plans, intentions and predictions | Select appropriate future forms for a plan, intention or prediction. | will, going to, present continuous |
| 2 | Consequences and conditions | Explain likely results of realistic conditions. | First conditional, unless, when/as soon as |
| 3 | Imagined situations | Describe an unreal or unlikely present/future situation and its result. | Second conditional |
| 4 | Choosing between options | Compare options and justify a decision using conditions and consequences. | Modals, comparison, reasoning |

**Checkpoint:** Solve a familiar decision-making task and explain the chosen option and likely consequences.

## Unit 5 — Media, culture and connected communication

**Unit outcome:** Understand the main points of clear standard input on familiar topics and summarise or respond with connected language.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Understanding longer texts | Identify main ideas, supporting details and the writer's basic purpose. | Reading strategies, reference words, cohesion |
| 2 | Reporting what someone said | Report statements and questions in common contexts. | Reported speech, tense/pronoun/time shifts where required |
| 3 | Comparing perspectives | Summarise similarities and differences between two familiar viewpoints. | Contrast/linking expressions, paraphrase |
| 4 | A connected presentation | Deliver or write a short structured presentation with an introduction, supporting points and conclusion. | Signposting, cohesion, pronunciation/organisation |

**Checkpoint:** Integrated reading/listening and a short summary or presentation. Avoid testing specialist cultural knowledge instead of English proficiency.

## B1 assessment blueprint

Each unit checkpoint should assess connected comprehension and meaningful production. Include a balance of receptive tasks, grammar/vocabulary in context and a short productive response. The final path review should sample narrative control, opinion/reasoning, problem-solving, future/hypothetical language and connected discourse. Rubrics must distinguish language accuracy from task achievement, coherence and interaction.
