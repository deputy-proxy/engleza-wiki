# B2 learning path: Effective and Nuanced Communication

**CEFR target:** B2  
**Entry assumption:** The learner can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity and produce clear detailed text, but needs greater control of complex discourse, nuance, register and argument.  
**Exit outcome:** The learner can understand the main ideas of complex texts on both concrete and abstract topics, interact with fluency and spontaneity, and produce clear, detailed text explaining viewpoints and evaluating options.  
**Path structure:** 5 units × 4 lessons; one reviewed unit checkpoint per unit.

## Unit 1 — Complex narratives and precise time relationships

**Unit outcome:** Produce detailed narratives and explanations with controlled tense/aspect choices and clear emphasis.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tense and aspect in extended narratives | Maintain clear time relationships across a multi-paragraph narrative. | Past perfect, past simple/continuous, narrative sequencing |
| 2 | Present perfect forms and emphasis | Choose between present perfect simple and continuous to express result, duration and activity. | Present perfect simple/continuous |
| 3 | Emphasis and inversion in context | Recognise and use selected emphatic structures where they are natural and purposeful. | Cleft sentences, limited negative adverbial inversion |
| 4 | Reconstructing an account | Summarise and retell a complex account while preserving chronology and emphasis. | Cohesion, reference, paraphrase |

**Checkpoint:** Analyse a detailed narrative and produce a coherent retelling with controlled time reference.

## Unit 2 — Argument, evidence and critical response

**Unit outcome:** Develop a clear argument, evaluate evidence and respond to counterarguments fairly.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Building a sustained argument | Organise a multi-paragraph argument with a clear thesis and logical progression. | Thesis, topic sentences, cohesive devices |
| 2 | Qualifying claims and evaluating evidence | Distinguish fact, interpretation and speculation and qualify claims appropriately. | Hedging, stance, evidential language |
| 3 | Counterarguments and concessions | Acknowledge and respond to an opposing argument without losing the main position. | Concession, contrast, complex clauses |
| 4 | Writing an evidence-based essay | Produce a structured essay with relevant support, a counterargument and a reasoned conclusion. | Register, cohesion, argument structure |

**Checkpoint:** Evaluate a short set of sources and produce a reasoned response. The rubric must assess reasoning and source use separately from language control.

## Unit 3 — Professional communication and register

**Unit outcome:** Adapt language and organisation to audience, purpose and level of formality.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Register and tone | Identify and revise language that is too informal, direct or vague for a professional context. | Register, collocation, politeness |
| 2 | Diplomatic requests and negotiation | Make proposals, qualify disagreement and negotiate a practical outcome. | Modals, indirect questions, diplomatic phrasing |
| 3 | Reports and recommendations | Summarise findings and make a supported recommendation in a structured report. | Passive forms, reporting verbs, formal organisation |
| 4 | Presenting a proposal | Deliver a structured proposal and respond to follow-up questions. | Signposting, emphasis, spontaneous interaction |

**Checkpoint:** Produce a professional written recommendation and handle a short follow-up discussion. Assess audience awareness and clarity.

## Unit 4 — Nuance, stance and complex grammar

**Unit outcome:** Express degrees of certainty, obligation, criticism and hypothetical meaning with appropriate nuance.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deduction and speculation | Make and evaluate deductions about present and past situations. | Modal verbs of deduction, modal perfect forms |
| 2 | Regret, criticism and alternatives | Discuss past alternatives, regret and criticism accurately. | Third/mixed conditionals, wish/if only |
| 3 | Relative and participle clauses | Condense information while maintaining clarity and correct reference. | Defining/non-defining relatives, participle clauses |
| 4 | Choosing precise structures | Edit a text to improve precision, emphasis and sentence variety without obscuring meaning. | Complex syntax, cohesion, editing |

**Checkpoint:** Interpret nuanced statements and produce a response that uses appropriate degrees of certainty and hypothetical meaning.

## Unit 5 — Complex input, synthesis and interaction

**Unit outcome:** Synthesise information from multiple sources and participate effectively in extended discussion.

| Order | Lesson | Measurable objective | Target language and skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reading for argument and implication | Identify argument structure, implied stance and relevant supporting detail in a complex text. | Inference, discourse markers, lexical nuance |
| 2 | Listening to extended contributions | Follow a sustained discussion and distinguish main claims from examples and qualifications. | Listening for stance, emphasis and signposting |
| 3 | Synthesising multiple sources | Combine information from two sources into a coherent, attributed summary. | Paraphrase, reporting verbs, synthesis |
| 4 | Leading a structured discussion | Develop a position, invite contributions, respond to challenge and summarise an outcome. | Turn-taking, repair, concession, spontaneous fluency |

**Checkpoint:** Read and listen to related source material, produce an attributed synthesis and take part in a structured discussion.

## B2 assessment blueprint

Each unit checkpoint should use complex but accessible input and require learners to interpret, evaluate or produce connected discourse. Include listening and reading inference, vocabulary/collocation in context, grammar choices in authentic discourse and productive tasks. The final review should assess sustained argument, register, nuanced stance, synthesis and interaction. Do not assign B2 merely because a task is long or its topic is abstract; verify the actual linguistic and cognitive demand.
