# CEFR content standards

Use the CEFR Companion Volume descriptors as the reference for communicative ability. A label such as A2 or B1 is not sufficient evidence on its own: reviewers must inspect the language and task demand.

| Level | Typical learner capability | Editorial implications |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-A1 | Recognises familiar words and very simple phrases with strong support | Concrete vocabulary, short instructions, visual/contextual support, highly controlled practice |
| A1 | Understands and uses familiar everyday expressions and basic phrases | Simple present, basic questions/negation, familiar contexts, short sentences and explicit scaffolding |
| A2 | Communicates in routine tasks and describes immediate needs and familiar matters | Common past/future forms, everyday transactional language, short connected texts and predictable exchanges |
| B1 | Deals with most familiar situations and produces simple connected text | Wider tense/aspect contrasts, reasons and opinions, connected paragraphs, less predictable contexts |
| B2 | Understands main ideas of complex text and interacts with fluency and detail | Nuance, argument, abstract topics, more complex syntax and sustained written/spoken tasks |
| C1 | Uses language flexibly and effectively for social, academic and professional purposes | Implicit meaning, register, precision, complex discourse and stylistic choices |
| C2 | Understands virtually everything and expresses fine shades of meaning precisely | Subtle nuance, idiomaticity, rhetorical effect and highly demanding texts/tasks |

## Assignment rules

- Assign a level based on the hardest essential language/task demand, not the topic label or a single easy question.
- Separate receptive and productive demands. A short A2 reading text does not make an open-ended B2 writing task A2.
- Record the primary skill and only add secondary skills when the activity actually assesses them.
- Topic tags describe the language feature or subject assessed; do not use broad taxonomy as a substitute for an objective.
- Tests must use a level-appropriate blueprint and a deliberate difficulty distribution. Reusing the same undifferentiated question pool across A1–B2 is not acceptable evidence of level alignment.
- Review adapted texts for lexical density, sentence complexity, cohesion, inference demand and cultural assumptions.
- Keep English examples, prompts and answer options in English when the learner is practising English. Translate instructions and explanatory support where appropriate.

Reviewers should cite the relevant CEFR descriptor or internal curriculum objective in the audit notes for borderline assignments.
